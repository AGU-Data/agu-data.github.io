# Global Data Resilience

**Preserving data as a world heritage to secure our future.**

Global Data Resilience (GDR) is building a consortium of repositories, repository networks, and supporting organizations that share capacity, coordinate effort, and act as a collective safety net for scientific data.

## About

Scientific data face threats on several fronts: U.S. repository closures, lost EU project funding, geopolitical conflict, natural hazards, and the loss of expert staff. Repositories are generally ready for routine operations but much less prepared for systemic shocks. GDR exists to change that, before the next crisis hits.

**Vision:** All impactful Earth, space, and environmental sciences data are persistently available through and beyond periods of disruption.

**Mission:** Ensure the long-term resilience of these data by establishing a sustainable federated network of repositories sharing capacity, coordinating effort, and building a collective safety net.

### How it started

- **A cascading problem.** A funding cut leads to lost staff and expertise, which leaves infrastructure unmaintained or off the grid, which deepens fragility. Fixing only one link in that chain still leaves repositories exposed.
- **A global response.** AGU launched the Data Resilience Project in summer 2025. In March 2026, it brought nearly 100 experts from around the world to Berlin, with support from the William and Flora Hewlett Foundation.
- **A shared roadmap.** Eleven task groups formed at the convening. Their work became the GDR roadmap, which lays out four tracks of work across five phases. The first set of pilots have launched in Oct/Nov 2026. 

> "Data resilience is not a plan. Data resilience is skilled people, tested specifications, and repositories that already know each other."
>
> — GDR Roadmap, Executive Summary

### What we are building

- **A governance body:** Lightweight coordination that guides the roadmap, links related efforts, and advocates for support.
- **A mutual support network:** A global network of networks for backup support, replication, and continuity help when a repository is in trouble.
- **Crisis preparedness guidance:** A Crisis Preparedness Kit, data delivery package specs, risk assessment tools, and scenario playbooks.
- **An interoperability recommendation:** Common standards and vocabulary mappings so data can move between different repositories.
- **An ethics and sovereignty guidance:** Expert advice on the legal and ethical side of moving data, including Indigenous data sovereignty.
- **A sustainability model:** A coalition-based financial structure that doesn't depend solely on government funding.
- **Outreach and education:** Growing the community of practice and delivering training so the guidance gets used.

**Resilience, defined:** "The ability to make important data and metadata accessible, recoverable, and useful under both normal operations and crisis conditions." (ESIP)

### Guiding principles

| Principle | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Federated, not centralized | Decisions made at the lowest appropriate level. |
| Lightweight | Low admin burden, easy to join, quick to coordinate. |
| Inclusive and multilingual | A real voice for Indigenous communities, the Global South, and small repositories. |
| Committed participation | Joining is voluntary; members contribute resources. |
| Built for resiliency | Works through crises without creating new single points of failure. |
| Shared values and trust | People and stewardship matter as much as technical systems. |
| Data sovereignty respected | Rights of owners, nations, and Indigenous Peoples are honored. |
| Reciprocity | Mutual aid freely given, with fair attribution. |

## Roadmap

The approach is to build the guidance by putting it into practice. Pilots test structures and standards in real repositories, and what we learn shapes each next phase.

### How we got here

| When | Milestone |
| --- | --- |
| September 2025 | **Project launches.** AGU creates the Data Resilience Project. |
| March 2026, Berlin | **Global convening.** Over 100 experts draft the first roadmap and form 11 task groups. |
| April-September 2026 | **Task group work.** Task groups develop plans for interoperability, governance, rights, and more. |
| October 2026 | **Roadmap compiled.** The GDR Consortium roadmap brings the task group plans together. |
| November 2026 | **Roadmap v1 published.** The GDR Consortium roadmap published for broad distribution, comment, and a catalyst for expanding engagement. |

### Five phases ahead

| Phase | Key work |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Build Foundation | Confirm core collaborators and teams; choose first pilot participants; inventory existing standards; hire a community manager and form an advisory board |
| 2. Pilot and Refine | ★ Crisis Preparedness Guideline v1.0; ★ Interoperability framework v1.0; pilots with three or more repositories; establish global coordination with the help of RDA and others |
| 3. Operate | ★ Operational governance body; ★ Mirroring and matching between repositories; ★ Replication built into preparedness guidance; open enrollment in the network |
| 4. Assess and Revise | Midterm review of every product; community assessment workshop; a new roadmap for the years ahead |
| 5. Grow and Maintain | Sustain, maintain, and grow the consortium; Guideline and framework v2.0 updates |

## Tracks and Pilots

Four tracks run in parallel and inform each other. Each has a working group, and pilots in real data communities put the work into practice.

### Track 1: Preparedness and Response

Make sure data are safeguarded through a mutual support network with clear crisis preparedness, response, and interoperability requirements.

- Crisis Preparedness Guideline for repositories
- Interoperability framework for resilience and AI readiness
- Distributed, replicated archiving pilots
- Repositories mirroring each other's data and services

*Working groups: Preparedness WG and Mutual Support Network WG*

### Track 2: Governance and Collaboration

Engage the community, run the consortium, and coordinate with related efforts worldwide. Coordination starts within the RDA.

- Set up the governance body
- Rules of engagement for the mutual support network
- Landscape tracking of related initiatives
- Outreach, education, and continuous improvement

*Working group: Governance WG; AGU coordinates landscaping, outreach, and administration*

### Track 3: Sustainability Models and Partner Development

Find lasting business and organizational models for the consortium and its repositories, with a broad range of partners.

- Initial fundraising and planning
- Industry roundtable, launched at Climate Week NYC 2026
- Design and test repository business models, including AI-ready data

*Working group: Business Model WG*

### Track 4: Rights

Guidance and support on legal, ethical, and sovereignty issues so data can be safeguarded across borders.

- Legal and ethical advisory framework for transferring data
- Indigenous advisory structure, building on CARE principles
- Organizational interoperability work with CODATA and RDA

*Working group: Rights WG, Legal WG*

### Initial pilots

| Pilot | Track | Focus |
| --- | --- | --- |

| Environmental data | Track 3: Business models | Exploring a range of business and organizational models for environmental data repositories. |
| Hydrologic data | Track 3: Business models | Exploring a range of business and organizational models for hydrologic data repositories. |

### Now: Dataset risk assessment for precipitation

Track 1 includes a pilot to build a repeatable way to assess and prioritize datasets by risk. AGU and the New York Climate Exchange, in cooperation with the Keeling Curve Foundation, are testing it on precipitation, a field where many records feed one another. Experts will score datasets at a workshop on December 15 and 16, 2026.

Example Datasets: GHCN-Daily · CoCoRaHS · US Climate Reference Network · GPCP · IMERG
